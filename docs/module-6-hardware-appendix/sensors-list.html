3. Sensors List and Specifications

Overview​

A humanoid robot requires a diverse array of sensors to perceive its environment, maintain balance, and interact safely with humans and objects. This chapter details the essential sensors for humanoid robotics applications, including specifications, mounting considerations, and integration requirements.

Vision Sensors​

RGB Cameras​

Essential for visual perception and recognition tasks:

Specifications:

  • Resolution: 720p to 4K (1080p recommended for most applications)
  • Frame Rate: 30–60 FPS (higher for dynamic tasks)
  • Field of View: 60–120 degrees depending on application
  • Interface: USB 3.0, GigE, or MIPI CSI-2
  • Mounting: Head-mounted for forward vision, additional cameras for 360° coverage

Recommended Models:

  • Logitech C920/C922: Good quality, widely supported
  • FLIR Blackfly S: Industrial grade, excellent for robotics
  • Intel RealSense D400: Integrated depth sensing

RGB-D Cameras​

For 3D perception and depth estimation:

Specifications:

  • Depth Technology: Stereo vision or structured light
  • Depth Range: 0.3 m to 10 m for indoor applications
  • Accuracy: ≤1% error at 1 m distance
  • Resolution: 640×480 to 1024×768 depth
  • IR Illumination: Active IR for low-light conditions

Recommended Models:

  • Intel RealSense D435/D435i: Excellent ROS 2 support, IMU integration
  • Intel RealSense D455: Higher resolution and accuracy
  • Azure Kinect: High-quality depth sensing
  • Orbbec Astra Pro: Cost-effective alternative

Wide-Angle / Fisheye Cameras​

For extended field of view and mapping:

Specifications:

  • Field of View: 180° or more for panoramic vision
  • Resolution: 1280×720 or higher
  • Distortion: Calibrated distortion parameters
  • Mounting: Multiple cameras for 360° coverage

LiDAR Sensors​

2D LiDAR​

For navigation and obstacle detection:

Specifications:

  • Range: 5–30 m depending on model
  • Accuracy: ≤2 cm at 10 m range
  • Resolution: 0.25°–1° angular resolution
  • Scan Rate: 5–20 Hz
  • Interface: Ethernet, USB, or serial

Recommended Models:

  • Hokuyo UAM-05LP: Reliable, well-supported
  • SICK TIM551: Industrial grade
  • YDLidar X4: Cost-effective option

3D LiDAR​

For complex environment mapping:

Specifications:

  • Range: 5–100 m depending on model
  • Channels: 16–128 beam options
  • Field of View: Vertical and horizontal FoV
  • Accuracy: ≤3 cm at 100 m
  • Weight: Critical for humanoid applications

Recommended Models:

  • Velodyne Puck / VLP-16: Good balance of performance and weight
  • Ouster OS0 / OS1: Solid-state, more robust
  • Livox Mid-360: Cost-effective 360° scanning

Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs)​

Body IMUs​

For balance and motion control:

Specifications:

  • Accelerometer: ±2 g to ±16 g range
  • Gyroscope: ±250°/s to ±2000°/s range
  • Magnetometer: For absolute orientation
  • Update Rate: 100–1000 Hz
  • Bias Stability: ≤10° per hour for gyros

Recommended Models:

  • VectorNav VN-100: High-performance, ROS support
  • SparkFun IMU Breakout: Cost-effective options
  • Adafruit 9-DOF: Good for prototyping

Joint Position Sensors​

For precise joint control:

Specifications:

  • Encoder Type: Absolute or incremental
  • Resolution: 12–24 bit for absolute encoders
  • Accuracy: ≤0.1° for precise control
  • Interface: CAN, EtherCAT, or SPI

Tactile Sensors​

Force / Torque Sensors​

For manipulation and interaction:

Specifications:

  • Axes: 6-axis (force and torque in 3D)
  • Range: Based on application requirements
  • Resolution: Sub-Newton force resolution
  • Update Rate: 100–1000 Hz
  • Interface: EtherCAT, CAN, or analog

Recommended Models:

  • ATI F/T Sensors: High-precision, industrial grade
  • Robotiq Force/Torque Sensor: Good for manipulation
  • Wacoh-Tech: Cost-effective options

Tactile Skin​

For contact detection and manipulation:

Specifications:

  • Resolution: Number of sensing elements
  • Sensitivity: Detection threshold for light touch
  • Update Rate: 100–500 Hz
  • Coverage: Areas requiring contact feedback

Audio Sensors​

Microphone Arrays​

For voice command processing:

Specifications:

  • Number of Microphones: 2–8 for beamforming
  • Sampling Rate: 16–48 kHz
  • Frequency Response: 20 Hz – 20 kHz
  • Signal-to-Noise Ratio: ≥60 dB for good audio quality

Recommended Configurations:

  • ReSpeaker Mic Array: Ready-to-use solutions
  • Custom USB microphones: Flexible positioning
  • Analog microphone arrays: For custom integration

Speakers​

For audio feedback and interaction:

Specifications:

  • Power: 5–20 W depending on environment
  • Frequency Response: 200 Hz – 20 kHz
  • Connectivity: USB, Bluetooth, or direct audio output

Safety and Environmental Sensors​

Collision Detection​

For safe operation:

Specifications:

  • Type: Contact switches, proximity sensors, or vision-based
  • Coverage: All potential collision points
  • Response Time: ≤10 ms for safety systems
  • Durability: Ruggedized for robot operation

Environmental Sensors​

For awareness of surroundings:

Specifications:

  • Temperature: Monitor internal temperatures
  • Humidity: Environmental monitoring
  • Gas Sensors: For safety in certain environments
  • Pressure: Barometric pressure for altitude estimation

Sensor Integration and Mounting​

Mounting Considerations​

  • Vibration Isolation: Protect sensitive sensors
  • Accessibility: Easy maintenance and calibration
  • Cable Management: Organized and protected connections
  • Weight Distribution: Balance sensor placement on robot

Power and Data Requirements​

  • Power Budget: Calculate total power consumption
  • Data Bandwidth: Ensure sufficient network capacity
  • Synchronization: Coordinate sensor timing
  • Redundancy: Critical sensors may need backup

Sensor Fusion and Processing​

Processing Requirements​

  • Computational Load: Each sensor adds processing requirements
  • Latency: Real-time processing for control applications
  • Synchronization: Coordinate data from multiple sensors
  • Calibration: Regular calibration procedures

Integration Architecture​

  • Sensor Hub: Centralized processing node
  • Distributed Processing: Edge processing on sensor modules
  • ROS 2 Integration: Standard message types and interfaces
# Example sensor integration
class SensorManager:
    def __init__(self):
        self.cameras = []
        self.lidar = None
        self.imus = []
        self.microphones = []

    def initialize_sensors(self):
        self.cameras.append(RGBDCamera(
            device_id="/dev/video0",
            resolution=(1280, 720),
            fps=30
        ))

        self.imus = [
            IMUSensor(node_id="head_imu"),
            IMUSensor(node_id="torso_imu"),
            IMUSensor(node_id="left_foot_imu"),
            IMUSensor(node_id="right_foot_imu")
        ]

        self.lidar = LiDARSensor(
            device_type="2d",
            range_max=10.0,
            resolution=0.25
        )
